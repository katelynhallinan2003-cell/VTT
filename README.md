# Table: a small virtual tabletop for friends

A browser-based VTT for 5e-style games: upload a map at full resolution, set a grid, drop tokens, reveal fog of war, and play
together with a link. Built for one DM and a handful of trusted friends, hosted free on GitHub Pages + Firebase + imgbb.

**Status: Phase 1 (playable core).** See the roadmap below.

## What works now

- **Maps** at full resolution (up to 8192 px a side on screen), uploaded to imgbb, kept in your library. In **New scene**, the library shows your latest pictures with a search box; **Show all** opens it out to every picture in a wider window, and pointing at one shows it larger. Pictures are added by uploading or dragging them in (there is no paste-a-link box). Removing a picture from the library also asks ImgBB to delete it; if ImgBB does not answer from the browser, its private deletion link stays under **ImgBB cleanup** in My library.
  **Drag a picture onto the page** to use it as a map: on a table with no scenes yet it opens the New scene box with the picture in it, and the New scene box takes a picture dropped anywhere while it is open (it shows a small preview). Once a table has scenes, a picture dropped on the board makes a token, as before (and one dropped while Add token is open becomes that token's picture); the page says which it will be while you are dragging.
- **Lock the map** so box-selecting and clicking never grab it. Unlock it to move or resize it.
- **Grid overlay:** show/hide, colour, opacity, feet per square, 5e / 5-10-5 / true-distance diagonals.
  Set it by **drawing a box over a square (or many squares)** or with sliders for size and offset.
- **Hex grids:** in the Scene tab, **Grid type** offers **Squares**, **Hex, rows** (point up), **Hex, columns** (flat top), and **Gridless**. If a new map has no clear printed grid, the scene starts gridless. Click two points ten feet apart and choose an overlay. To recalibrate an existing map, use **Set scale with two points**. Everything follows the hexes: tokens snap to the middle of a hex (a Large creature to the corner where three hexes meet, a Huge one to a middle again), arrow keys step to the neighbouring hex (up and down zigzag so they stay in line), the ruler and the drag readout count hexes (every step is 5 ft, with no diagonals to argue about, and climbing counts like it does on squares), spell templates snap to hex middles, corners and sides and light up the hexes they cover, and "Draw a box to set the grid" works around a row (or column) of hexes. The size is the distance between the middles of two neighbouring hexes. Switching back to squares is one click, and Ctrl+Z undoes a change of type.
- **Tokens:** image upload, circle/square/frameless, ring colour and thickness, sizes from Tiny to Gargantuan, snapping (hold Alt to skip),
  box and shift selection, nudging, duplicate (with auto-numbering), z-order, name labels, drag distance readout.
- **Live play:** one shared link for players. They pick a name and colour, then claim a token (or make their own).
- **Permissions:** Trusting (default) / Standard / Strict presets plus individual toggles.
- **Hidden tokens:** DM-only tokens that players cannot even download. Reveal and hide at will.
- **Fog of war:** manual, VBM-style. Reveal or hide with boxes, free-form shapes or a brush. Undo, hide all, reveal all, and preview as players: the **eye** button in the toolbar (between the grid and fog buttons, or **O**) shows the map the way your players see it, and the same button takes you back.
- **Pings:** middle-click the map or a token (configurable in Settings → Mouse and keys: you can swap pan and ping buttons, or ping by holding the left mouse button).
- **Undo / redo:** Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y for token moves, edits, deletes, map moves, grid changes and fog.
- **Themes:** light (white drawing surface) and dark, plus a few fun ones, and your own accent colour, all in Settings → You.
- **Drawing:** pen, line, arrow, box, circle, text and an eraser. Shapes and text stay editable: click one to select it, then change its colours (outline and fill are separate; fill can be none, see-through or solid), thickness, dashing, or type a width / height / length in feet. Drag the white dots to resize, drag the shape to move it, arrow keys nudge it, Delete removes it. Text is typed right on the map at the size it will have, and can be edited again (double-click it, or select it and press Enter): font, size, bold, italic, underline, colour. Everyone with the permission can draw; you can change or delete your own (the DM can touch anything).
- **Spell templates (2024 rules):** cone, cube, sphere, emanation and line that snap to your grid and read out their size in feet. A cone starts at the middle of a square's edge, an emanation is built around the creature it starts on (it picks up the token in the middle automatically) and is counted from the edge of that creature's space, so a Large or bigger creature's 10 ft emanation reaches 10 ft past its whole footprint, and its own space stays clear, cubes line up with whole squares, a line starts at the nearer of a square's side middle or a corner, and a sphere snaps to the middle of a square (or a corner, or nowhere, your choice). The squares an area covers light up (a square counts when its middle is inside); turn that off in the template panel. Hold Alt for free placement. Templates are sticky: the squares at the edge no longer flicker when the pointer sits on a boundary or wiggles at one distance (a 20 ft sphere always keeps its 49 squares), and cones, cubes and lines pull onto neat 15° angles. A template can **count as difficult ground** (Spike Growth, Entangle, a web): switch on "Counts as difficult ground" in the template panel before placing it, or select a template and use the switch on its bar. The squares it covers (the ones the spell can reach, so a wall that stops the spell stops this too) are hatched like any difficult ground, and a walk through them costs the extra square, in the drag readout and in a test move, for as long as the template is on the map; flyers are not slowed. **Picking a template:** the template panel has **Place** and **Select**. In Select mode a click picks the template under the pointer (Shift adds more), dragging moves it a square at a time (Alt: freely), its dots resize it, and **Delete** (or the Delete button on its bar) removes just that one; Ctrl+Z brings it back. Place mode is as before, and **Clear all templates** still empties the scene.
- **Ruler:** measure with your table's diagonal rule, in any colour you like. Private until you choose to show it. Right-click (or Esc) clears it. Turn on "Measure in several steps" and each drag adds a leg that carries on from the last, with the distance so far at every corner, the end of the first leg included (the 5-10-5 rule counts along the whole path); right-click takes back the last leg and Shift+drag starts a new measurement. Switching on test move turns the ruler off, and also puts away whichever other tool you had open.
- **Test move:** try a move without making it. The token shows at the new spot, a shadow stays behind, with the distance. Keep it or reset it, and right-click goes back one step. Turn on "Multi-step" and each drag adds a leg to the move (the dashed path and the whole distance stay on screen) instead of replacing it. Others only see it if you switch on "Show others".
- **Auras:** coloured areas around a token (radius in feet, from the edge or the centre, round or square, DM-only if you like). "On grid" (the default) fills whole squares like a spell template; switch it off for a smooth shape.
- **Initiative:** the d20 button in the left menu (or **I**) opens a small pop-up with a Roll tab and an Order tab. On the Roll tab every token has a **Bonus** box (remembered for that token, also in the token editor), a **d20** box and a 🎲 button, and the total is d20 plus bonus. Use the digital dice (**Roll all empty** rolls every box still empty) or type what a real die showed, then **Add N to the order**. The round counter and the ◀ / **Next turn ▶** buttons only appear once there is an order to step through. The order sorts itself, then you step through turns and rounds (◀ goes back and puts effect timers back too), drag lines to reorder, and hide any line from players. "Add something else" puts things like a lair action or a monster they have not met on your tracker only (tick "Hide from players"). End combat has no confirmation; Ctrl+Z brings the order back. The order itself shows as little boxes across the top middle of the screen (Settings can move it to a list down the right edge). Players see it once you tick "Show to players", and their own tokens are underlined.
- **Effects and conditions:** select a token and press **Effects** at the bottom (or right-click it). It opens as a small **window you can drag around**, so you can watch the token while you decide: the board behind stays usable, and whatever you hover over, arrow onto or pick shows on the token straight away, before anything is added. By default the view jumps so the token is on the left and the window on the right (zooming in a little if the token is tiny); a setting (in the window itself and in Settings) switches that to "stay where I was", which leaves both the view and the window where they were. **Ready-made** is a searchable drop-down of about 50 effects in groups: the 5e conditions, combat states (Raging, Dodging, cover…), spell effects (Bane, Hex, Hunter's Mark, Faerie Fire…) and a few others. **Make your own** takes any name, a colour and a picture chosen from a pop-up grid of emoji (or type or paste any emoji); the ones you make are kept in the list under "Yours". Choose how it looks on the token (a round badge, a glow, symbols orbiting the token, or a dot), whether it also **tints the whole token** (the colour follows the effect's colour unless you pick another), and how long it lasts: until you remove it, or a number of rounds that counts down at the end of its turn, the start of its turn, or every round as you press Next turn. The effects already on the token are listed at the top of the window, each with a tint switch, a pencil to change it, and a ✕. When time runs out it goes away by itself and you get a note. With no turn order running it just stays, and you can nudge the timer with − and +. Everyone who can see the token sees its effects (hover the little badges in the order for names and time left).
- **Hit points:** set on a token in the editor, shown as a bar on the token, in the initiative list and in the box at the bottom. Type a number and press − or + (Enter hurts, `+5` then Enter heals). Bloodied (half) and near death (a tenth) colour the token's edge. You choose what players see per token: nothing, colour only, or a bar. **Temp HP** are a separate pool: they soak up damage first, healing never touches them, and they can go past the maximum (type a number and press Temp next to the − / + buttons, or use the editor; it tells you what it did, and keeps the higher of the old and new amounts). **Several tokens at once:** select them (Shift, Ctrl or Cmd-click on the board or in the Tokens tab, or drag a box) and press **Group HP** on the token bar: type one number, pick **Damage**, **Heal** or **Temp HP**, untick anyone who is not hit, mark **½** for the ones that made their saving throw (they take half, rounded down), and press Apply. Each row shows what that token will have afterwards, and one Ctrl+Z takes the whole thing back. Players never receive the numbers, only a percentage, and the real values sit in the DM-only part of the database. Torches and other light-only marks (a light with no picture and no frame) are never in the initiative list; a creature carrying a light is.
- **Sight and fog:** give a token a sight distance (in feet) and, with "Tokens clear the fog around them" on, the fog opens around it as it moves. "Remember what they have seen" keeps the ground behind them, following the route the token really took (it needs your browser open, because it is the DM's browser that writes it down). The fog then has **three kinds of ground**, as in most virtual tabletops: **black** where nobody has been, **grey** where they have looked before but cannot see this moment (a monster standing there is out of sight until someone looks again, and cannot be clicked), and **clear** where a token sees right now. "Grey out what they cannot see right now" switches the grey off, and then remembered ground simply stays clear. Putting fog back over a place (the Fog tool) makes it unseen again. Hold **Q** while dragging a token to teleport it (or right-click empty ground and choose "Teleport selected token here"): only where it lands is revealed. An undo or other sudden jump counts as a teleport too. Hidden tokens never clear fog, and a test move does not peek ahead.
- **Hide the grid printed on a map, and use it as your grid:** many map pictures come with a grid already drawn on them, which clashes with the app's own. **A map you add is looked at once, automatically** (square or hex, whichever is clearly there; switch this off under Scene → Grid printed on the map → "Look for a printed grid when I add a map"). When lines are clearly there they are covered and **your own grid is set on them** (size, shape and where it starts), so you never have to draw the grid; one Ctrl+Z puts both back. **Find and hide it** does the same on demand, and also brings up grids it was not sure enough of to set up by itself (a grid in a few small rooms of a mostly black map, or very faint lines). The finder is built for real maps: it only counts thin lines (not walls, shadows or the border of the map), and a line has to show in many parts of the picture, so one strong wall cannot outvote the grid. A grid drawn only on the floor of the rooms, as dashes, or as little crosses at the corners is still found, under furniture, on a picture saved as a JPEG, and on very large pictures. It tells squares from hexes and point-up from flat-top, and the size of the grid the scene happened to have makes no difference. It leaves alone a tiled floor in one room, patterns finer than any battle grid, and anything it is unsure of. The lines are located to a fraction of a pixel (the spacing is fitted to where every line really is, so lines far from the first one stay on target on a big picture) and covered by filling each in from an **average of the ground just outside it on both sides**, fading out at the edges so no seam shows; hexes the same way. The picture itself is never changed: it is done on screen, from a few saved numbers, so players see the same thing and **Hide the map's grid lines** switches it back at any time. If you move your grid by hand, **Line my grid up with it** puts it back on the printed one. Open **Grid printed on the map** to widen the covered strip (**Line width**) or nudge it (hexes also get a size slider). It works best on lines that are a few shades different from the ground (very faint ones are left alone).
- **A grid instead of a map:** a scene does not need a picture. **New scene → Just a grid** (or **Start with a grid** on a brand new table's first-scene card) makes a page of squares you choose the size of (30 by 20 to start), and **Scene → Map → Just a grid** does the same to a scene that has no map yet; the size can be changed there later. The page is dark paper with your grid on it, and fog, walls, light and tokens all work as usual.
- **More maps in the same scene:** Scene → Map → **Add a second map…** (then **Add another map…**, up to four extra) opens one window with **Upload map…**, **From your library** (a click adds that picture at once, with a search box once there are many) and **Add an empty grid** (a number of squares across and down, put beside the map on the grid lines). Each map has its own name, **Players can see it** switch (hidden ones show faded to you only), **Locked in place** and **Size** (squares, for an empty grid), and its own grid: **Show the grid here** (a switch apart from the scene's), **Square size here** (so a map drawn at another scale gets squares to match; the scene's grid is left out of that patch and its first line sits on the map's top left corner), **Grid colour here** and, for an empty grid, **Paper colour** (each with a way back to "the same as the scene"). Tokens on such a map are as big as its squares and snap to them, and the ruler and light and sight distances count in them; feet per square, the diagonal rule and square or hex stay the scene's, and spell templates, heights, painted ground, walls snapping and fog still use the scene's grid; while it is unlocked you drag it on the board to where it belongs, and Ctrl+Z undoes a move, a resize or a removal. A new upload shows what it is doing ("Uploading…", "Adding…") and can be kept in your library. The view moves so the new picture is clear of the side panel. Fog, the grid, light and the walls all cover the area of every map together. Maps are stored in the scene, so they come along when you export a campaign.
- **Walls, doors and windows:** the **Walls** tool (brick icon, **B**) draws lines that block things, so fog and spells follow the real shape of a dungeon. **Wall** stops sight, walking and spells. **Door** blocks all three while it is closed; click its white diamond (in any tool) to open or close it. **Window** lets sight and spells through but not walking while it is shut; click its small square to open it, and then tokens can walk through too (windows start shut). Draw **Lines** (click points and the walls run on until you double-click, press Enter or Esc, or right-click, or drag one at a time; coming back to where you began closes the loop), a **Box** for a whole room, a **Round** room (drag from the middle out to the edge), or **Freehand**: draw the outline with the mouse held down and it is turned into straight walls with as few or as many corners as you pick (Few, Some, Many); come back to the start and it closes. Freehand and round walls are ordinary walls, so you can bend them afterwards. Ends snap onto other walls so rooms close up, then to grid corners (or corners and halves, or nowhere); **Shift** locks straight and 45° lines and **Alt** turns snapping off. A door or window drawn along a plain wall **cuts a gap** in it, so you can draw a long wall first and drop the doors in afterwards. Every piece can be **drawn for the players or only for you**: plain walls start hidden (the map picture already shows them), doors and windows start visible, and hidden walls still do their job. In **Edit** mode click a wall (or drag a box) to select it, drag an end (every wall that meets there follows, so corners stay closed) or the wall itself, change its type, visibility, side or height, open a door, or press Delete; **double-click a wall to give it a corner** (drag the corner to bend it), or a corner where two walls meet to take it out; everything is undoable. A wall can be **one-way**, for a cliff edge: little ticks mark the side that is stopped (the bottom of the cliff), where a token just sees a wall and cannot walk through it, while a token on the other side (the top) sees and walks right past it. Draw it with the One-way switch on, or switch it on for selected walls; **X** (or the Flip button) swaps the stopped side. A wall can also have a **height** in feet above the ground it stands on (0 is too tall to get over): a flyer, or a token standing on ground higher than the wall's top, sees and moves over it, while anything lower does not. With "Tokens clear the fog around them" on, a token sees as far as its sight reaches **in straight lines**: a closed room is dark outside it, a doorway or window lets a beam through, and what a token explores as it walks is shaped the same way. **Walls stop the players' tokens** (a scene switch, on by default; it and the other two switches live under a folded **On this map** heading in the Walls tool) when they drag or use the arrow keys; the DM can turn on "DM can move tokens through walls", or hold **Q** while dragging (which teleports the token, ignoring walls). Spell templates only light the squares the point they start from can reach in a straight line (with "show the squares it covers" on); a wall that is lower than the ground the spell starts on does not stop it, so a sphere set off on top of a cliff spreads over the edge, while one set off below is stopped by the cliff. **Detect walls from the map…** (in the Walls tool) looks for the walls printed on the map picture and adds them for you to check. Click a piece of wall on the picture; if the colour draws thin lines the walls follow the lines, and if it fills the rock round the rooms (dark rock, light floors) they run along its edge, which is chosen for you and can be switched. How close the colour must be is worked out from the picture, and a rocky, ragged edge is smoothed into a few long walls. If the colour is not one flat colour (grass, shadow and sand, say) the window tells you the result is a guess and draws it coarse: on a painted map like that it is quicker to draw the walls by hand. The picture is drawn with the walls it found over it, with sliders for how close the colour must be and for ignoring short bits, a switch that ignores a thin printed grid, snapping of ends to grid corners, and a height (10 ft to start). **Add** puts them all in as plain walls in one step, so one Ctrl+Z takes them back. Doors, windows and one-way cliff edges are still set by hand, and painterly or cave maps usually need some tidying. Only the DM opens doors and windows for now. Walls are readable by players' browsers like fog is, so they are meant to keep honest players honest, not to hide secrets from someone who opens the developer tools.
- **Heights and flying:** for cliffs, nests and birds. With the **Heights** tool (mountain icon, **H**) you **paint** high or low ground like a brush, in feet (a 10 ft ledge, a 40 ft nest, a -10 ft pit). By default the brush fills whole squares (or hexes) and you pick its size (1 to 8 across); turn that off for a free round brush, or hold **Alt** to flip it for one stroke. Everything you paint in one go is an **area**, and the next stroke keeps joining the selected area until you press **New area** (or **Esc**), so a big hill does not have to be painted in one drag. **Ctrl-click** an area to select it, then paint more onto it, **Erase** part of it (with an area selected it only erases from that one, otherwise from everything), or switch to **Select** mode, where one click picks an area and the card shows its height to change; drag a selected area to move it (whole squares at a time, Alt for free); you can also change its height in the card, **Merge** several into one, or **Delete** it. Areas stack in the order they were painted, newer on top. Each one is either **exact** ("this ground is 20 ft") or **added on top** of the ground under it ("raise it 5 ft"), so a 5 ft step on a 20 ft hill is 25 ft. The ground is drawn as a soft tint with a shadow on the low side of every drop and a thin line where the height changes; in the Heights tool each visible patch of ground shows its total elevation (for example, "25 ft") when the label fits. **The ground only shows when you need it** ("Show the ground heights" in the Heights panel and in Settings; it only changes your own screen): by default it fades in while you drag a token, measure with the ruler or step with the arrow keys, and fades out again. You can also pick Always, or only while the Heights tool is open. Boxes and circles drawn with the old version still work. Anything that **walks** onto ground is lifted to its height by itself (a small ▲ tag shows it, with a shadow); anything that **flies** keeps its own elevation, and its ↑ tag (and the "Above ground" box) says how far above the ground it is right now, so a flyer 20 ft up over flat ground is 30 ft up once it crosses ground that is 10 ft lower, without having changed its height; it is never pushed into the ground. Select a token and press **Height** at the bottom (or **]** to go up 5 ft and **[** to come down, which lands it at the ground), or set Flying and its height above the ground in the token editor. Players can change their own tokens' height too. The **ruler** and the distance shown while dragging count the climb in a straight 3D line using your diagonal rule ("35 ft (30 across, 20 up)"), and dragging a walker off an edge says how far it drops and how many d6 of falling damage that would be. **Paint height from a colour…** (Heights tool, Paint mode) is for maps where the picture already shows the difference: click the colour of the water, pit or platform on the picture, give it a height (−10 ft for water), and every square or hex that is mostly that colour is painted as one area. If the same height has more than one colour, press **+** and click the next colour on the picture (click a swatch to change that colour instead, ✕ to drop it). There are sliders for how close the colour must be and how much of a cell must match, a switch to take only the patch you clicked (on to start with; off takes the colours wherever they are on the map), and a preview on the picture; one Ctrl+Z takes it all back. A flat picture does not say how high things are, so you pick the height yourself. "Clear all" drawings leaves terrain alone; the Heights panel has its own "Clear all heights". **Ramp** mode paints ground that climbs (or falls) from a start height to an end height as you drag, in 5 ft steps (each step is an area of its own, so it can be edited like any other). **Stairs and cliffs:** a ground change of 5 ft or less forms a slope across five horizontal feet. A walker follows the slope, and the movement readout shows the change once as stairs. On gridless maps the distance follows the grade. On a grid, climbing or descending a stair costs like a diagonal move by your distance rule (on 5-10-5 every second one costs an extra square; on 5-5-5 nothing extra). A rise of more than 10 ft within 10 ft sideways (**Steepest climb** in the Heights panel; 0 turns it off) is a **cliff**: it is a wall for anything lower than the top of it (sight, spells and walking stop there) and nothing at all for whatever stands on top, who can walk off it and fall. **Difficult ground:** switch the Heights panel to "Difficult" and paint squares with the same brush; they are hatched on the map, a walker entering one pays an extra square of movement (the drag readout says "difficult ground +10 ft"), and flyers are not slowed.
- **Light and darkness:** in the Scene tab, **Light and darkness** sets how bright the scene is: Daylight (nothing changes), Twilight (everything dim, lights brighten the ground near them) or Dark (night or a dungeon). Lights are little flames **everyone can see** (**Place a light**: candle, torch, lamp, lantern, light spell, daylight spell, with bright and dim distances as in the rulebooks; make one hidden to keep it a secret), or a token can carry one. **Edit token → Sight and lights** is its own tab: how far it sees, a **Darkvision** switch with a distance (30, 60, 90, 120 or your own), and its light, which you can set to any bright and dim distance and colour and **save under a name** ("Everburning torch") to pick again later, also when placing lights from the Scene tab (kept in that browser). Walls stop light, and so does a closed door; windows let it through, and a light up high shines over low walls. In the dark, with fog and "Tokens clear the fog around them" on, a token sees only ground that a light reaches, plus what its darkvision covers (as if dim) and what is right beside it. Walking in the dark remembers (with "Keep what they have explored") what the token's own light, darkvision and touch reached, and any lit ground it could see from where it stood. A token hidden from the players gives no light. **A light goes through walls when you drag it** (walls still stop its light): lights are props you place and leave, so they are never held back by a wall or a cliff, while creatures are. A placed light is its own kind of token: its editor (double-click it, or **Edit** on its tag) has only the light, its name, hiding and locking, flying and height, and hit points (a light can be a spell effect), and its tag and menu leave out effects, auras, size, sight, initiative and the library. **Auto-add lights…** (next to Place a light) works out where lights belong and shows them on the map before it adds anything: a light on each lamp or torch drawn on the picture (a small, bright, warm spot that stands out from the floor round it), and, for each room the walls close off that has no light yet, enough lights that about nine tenths of it is lit with walls in the way (a light counts as reaching its bright distance plus half its dim distance). Pick the kind of light, switch either source off, then **Add** puts them all in as one step (one Ctrl+Z takes them back). A room needs walls that meet all the way round; rooms already lit by a light you placed are left alone. If the scene is in daylight, lights only show once it is Twilight or Dark. Your own view is see-through so you can still work on the map; **Preview as players** shows the real darkness. **Painted light** (same card): press **Paint light** and brush **Daylight** (full brightness, where the sun shines: the mouth of a cave, a courtyard open to the sky), **Dim light** (half as strong) or **Erase** straight onto the ground, with a brush from 5 to 80 ft. Like a lamp, painted light has a bright middle and a **Soft edge** that fades out beyond it, from full strength at the edge of the paint down to nothing (Hard, 10, 20, 40 or 80 ft, 20 to start; one setting for the whole scene), so it has no squares or lines in it. It shows only in a Twilight or Dark scene, token sight sees it, walls stop it just like a lamp's light (the glow does not leak through a wall; it spreads round it), it never changes height or movement, and "Clear all heights" leaves it alone. **Clear painted light** takes all of it away. The gold (daylight) and blue (dim) tint is only on your screen while you paint.
- **Bring players here:** the top-bar button sends every player's view to exactly what you are looking at, with a short glide, and tells them. Players who join later are not moved.
- **Summon players:** scenes you make stay private until you move the table onto them. On a private scene the top bar shows **Summon players** (it moves everyone there, and then **Bring players here** takes over for pointing their view at part of the map), and a short note, "Hidden from players", sits in the lower left corner. Once someone has joined the table, making a scene offers a tick for **Summon players to it as soon as it is made** (a first scene on a table nobody has joined is simply the live scene).
- **Scene folders:** next to the scene picker there is a **folder picker** (All scenes, your folders, Not in a folder, **+ New folder…**). Pick a folder and the scene list shows only its scenes, so a long campaign can be prepared in advance in tidy groups ("Act 1", "Waterdeep", "Random encounters"). A new scene starts in the folder that is showing (and the New scene box has a Folder choice). **Manage scenes and folders…** lists every folder with its scenes: move a scene with its folder choice, rename or delete a folder (its scenes are kept and become loose). Opening a scene in another folder (from a summon, the list, anywhere) moves the folder picker to it. Folders are saved with the table, and in the campaign file by name.
- **Saved tokens (DM):** select a token and press **Save to library** (or right-click → Save to library). It is kept with all its settings: picture and framing, size, shape, ring, auras, sight, darkvision, light, initiative bonus and its maximum hit points (not the damage it has taken or conditions, so each one you drop in is fresh). **Library → Saved tokens** lists them; **Add to board** puts one where you are looking, as many times as you like, in any scene. Your saved tokens belong to your account.
- **Characters (players) and tokens that travel:** a player presses **Save character** on their own token (or in **My token**). It is kept in their own library with its look and the conditions on it, and the token remembers which character it is. In **My token → Your characters** they can **Bring here** in any scene (once per scene), and the entry keeps itself up to date as the token changes (a condition the DM adds, a new picture). Hit points belong to the DM, so the DM's screen files each character's hit points and gives them back when that character arrives in another scene. When you **Summon players** to another scene, the players' tokens are copied there too, with their hit points and conditions, unless that character is already there (Settings → Table switches this off). A player's characters are kept **with the table** (not in their browser), under the identity they joined with. On a new browser or after clearing data, the join screen shows **Played here before? Pick your name**: picking it brings their characters back, and the DM's screen hands their old token to the new browser and tidies the old entry away once it is offline. The DM's saved tokens follow their Google account.
- **Handouts:** the top-bar **Handouts** button holds letters, notes and pictures you write ahead of time. **Show…** puts one in front of everyone or only the players you tick; it opens on their screens, and they can read it again later from their own **Handouts** button. **Hide** takes it back (you keep it), and changing one that is being shown updates it for the players quietly. Handouts shown to only some players are kept apart in the app, not in the database, so a determined player could still find the others in their browser tools. For real secrets, tell them out loud. Firebase needs the rules republished once for handouts (paste the new `database.rules.json` into the Rules tab of the console).
- **Campaign file:** **Settings → Pictures and files → Save campaign to a file** downloads one file with every scene (tokens including hidden ones and their hit points, walls, fog, drawings, initiative), your handouts, and optionally your map and token library. **Add a saved campaign…** puts such a file into any table you run: it lists what is inside first, then adds the scenes and handouts as new ones. Nothing already in the table is changed or replaced, scenes with the same name get "(2)", tokens that belonged to players come in unclaimed, and handouts come in not shown. Pictures are saved as links, so they come back as long as the links still work. The file contains your hidden things, so do not share it with players.
- **Token maker:** drag and zoom the picture, colour behind it, colour dropper, and a round-cursor brush with separate *Erase paint* (rubs out your paint) and *Erase image* (cuts parts out of the picture itself). **Auto select** picks an area by colour: click it, then drag away from the click to select more (or back to select less) and the selection updates live, or use the Sensitivity slider. Shift-click adds to the selection. Paint, Erase paint and Erase image then only work inside it, or outside it with *Invert*.
- **Scenes:** prepare a scene privately, then show it to players when ready. Tokens and fog are saved per scene. Switch, add and manage scenes from the one dropdown top left.
- **Where things are:** the toolbar on the left has the everyday tools first (select, add a token, ruler, then draw, spell templates and test move) and the DM's scene tools in a group below (grid, fog, walls, heights, initiative). **Settings** has four tabs: **Table** (name, player link, what players can do), **Pictures and files** (the imgbb key, the campaign file), **You** (name, colour, theme, what your own screen shows) and **Mouse and keys** (`?` opens it there). Notes, the test-move bar, warnings and the turn order hang from the top of the screen in one stack: between the two halves of the top bar when there is room, in a row under it on a smaller screen, so nothing covers anything else. **Scene → Grid** reads top to bottom: the grid type, Show grid, Snap tokens, Grid colour, **Fit the grid to the map** (find the printed one, set the scale with two points, or draw a box), and then three folded sections for the rarer things: Grid printed on the map, Fine tune grid and Distance rules.
- **Reloading the page:** the DM's browser comes back on the scene it was looking at (it is remembered per table in that browser), even when that is not the scene the players are on; if that scene has since been deleted it falls back to the live one.
- **Side tabs:** Scene, Tokens and Players are separate tabs down the right edge. They start folded away; click one to open it, click it again to fold it. The Scene tab opens by itself when you create your first scene. The grid is set from the Scene tab ("Draw a box to set the grid"); the toolbar button just shows or hides it. **Tokens:** under the list the players are shown as chips; click one to open that player's card with their **saved tokens** (Edit opens the same token editor, kept as the player's own copy; ✕ removes it) and the **handouts** that are theirs or for everyone (Edit opens the handout). **Players:** at the top is a plain **Player view** switch (the same as the eye button and **O**) that shows you the map the way your players see it.
- **Accounts:** Google sign-in for DMs only (allowlist), saved library of maps and token art that follows you between devices. On the start page each of **Your tables** can be renamed or deleted (deleting asks first, removes its scenes, players' saved characters and handouts, and cannot be undone; pictures on ImgBB are not touched). Deleting a table fully (so its link stops working) needs `database.rules.json` republished.

## How it's built

- **PixiJS (WebGL)** draws the board, so big maps and many tokens stay smooth. Tokens move locally the instant you drag and sync in small batches.
- **Preact** for the panels. **Firebase Realtime Database** for sync. Both backends (Firebase and the in-browser demo) share one small interface in `src/backend/`.
- `src/state/client.ts` is the single place that knows the database layout. `database.rules.json` (generated by `scripts/build-rules.mjs`) says who may read and write what.
